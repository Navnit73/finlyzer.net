# How to Prepare a Bank Reconciliation Statement

A bank reconciliation statement starts from two figures that rarely agree, the balance on the bank statement and the balance in your books, and adjusts each one until they do. The adjustments are the reconciling items.

## Adjusting the Bank Balance

Start from the closing balance on the bank statement, then:

- **Add deposits in transit:** money you recorded as received that the bank had not credited by the statement date.
- **Subtract outstanding cheques:** payments you recorded that had not cleared the bank yet.
- **Correct bank errors:** for example a deposit credited to the wrong account.

## Adjusting the Book Balance

Start from the cash or bank balance in your ledger, then:

- **Add bank credits you have not recorded:** interest earned, collections made by the bank, transfers in.
- **Subtract bank charges you have not recorded:** service fees, NSF (bounced) cheques, card fees and direct debits.
- **Correct book errors:** such as a payment of 540 entered as 450.

When the adjusted bank balance equals the adjusted book balance, the account is reconciled. Every adjustment on the book side needs a journal entry; the bank-side items normally clear on the next statement.
