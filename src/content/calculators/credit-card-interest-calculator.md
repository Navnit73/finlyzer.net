# How Much Interest Will My Card Charge This Month?

**Monthly interest ≈ balance × APR ÷ 365 × days in the billing cycle**

For a 30-day cycle on a 365-day basis:

| Balance | 20% APR | 24% APR | 29.99% APR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| $1,000 | $16.44 | $19.73 | $24.65 |
| $5,000 | $82.19 | $98.63 | $123.25 |
| $10,000 | $164.38 | $197.26 | $246.49 |

That charge is added to what you owe before your payment comes off. If your payment barely exceeds it, the balance hardly moves.

# How Is Credit Card Interest Calculated? A Statement Walk-Through

Issuers turn the APR into a **daily periodic rate** and charge it on your **average daily balance**:

1. **Daily periodic rate** = APR ÷ 365. At 24% that is 0.06575% a day. A few issuers divide by 360.
2. **Average daily balance** = the sum of each day's balance ÷ the days in the cycle.
3. **Interest for the cycle** = average daily balance × daily rate × days in the cycle.

Say you start a 30-day cycle owing $5,000 and pay $200 on day 13. The balance is $5,000 for 12 days and $4,800 for 18 days:

| Step | Working | Result |
|---|---|---:|
| Average daily balance | ($5,000 × 12 + $4,800 × 18) ÷ 30 | $4,880.00 |
| Interest for the cycle | $4,880 × 0.06575% × 30 | $96.26 |
| Same payment on the due date | $5,000 × 0.06575% × 30 | $98.63 |

Paying earlier in the cycle trimmed $2.37 off the charge. The calculator uses the balance at the start of each cycle, the simpler and slightly higher figure, so its estimates are on the cautious side.

# How Long Will It Take to Pay Off $5,000?

At 24% APR with no new spending:

| Monthly payment | Time to pay off | Total interest |
|---|---|---:|
| Minimum: 1% of balance + interest (at least $25) | 19 years 2 months | $8,598.27 |
| $150 | 4 years 7 months | $3,228.83 |
| $195.31 | 3 years | $2,030.75 |
| $200 | 2 years 11 months | $1,957.82 |
| $250 | 2 years 2 months | $1,421.84 |
| $300 | 1 year 9 months | $1,122.97 |
| $500 | 1 year | $625.07 |

Going from $150 to $300 a month saves $2,105.86 and almost three years. The first month's minimum on this card is $149.62; the trouble starts when you let it fall with the balance.

# Why Minimum Payments Take Decades

A typical minimum is a percentage of the balance plus the month's interest, with a floor such as $25. As the balance drops, so does the minimum, and the payoff slows to a crawl. Some cards charge a percentage only, without adding interest. At 24% APR a 2% percentage-only minimum barely beats the interest each month, and with a $25 floor the example card is still not repaid after 100 years.

Your card agreement states the rule; enter it under **Minimum payment rule** to see your real minimum-only cost next to the plan you choose.

# Is a 0% Balance Transfer Worth It?

A transfer swaps your APR for a promotional rate in exchange for a fee, usually a percentage of the amount moved. Whether it pays depends on how much is left when the promotion ends. For the same $5,000 at 24% APR, paying $200 a month:

| | Stay on the card | 3% fee, 0% for 18 months | 3% fee, 0% for 12 months | 5% fee, 0% for 21 months |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Transfer fee | | $150.00 | $150.00 | $250.00 |
| Owed when 0% ends | | $1,550.00 | $2,750.00 | $1,050.00 |
| Interest | $1,957.82 | $149.17 | $489.78 | $69.81 |
| Total cost | $1,957.82 | $299.17 | $639.78 | $319.81 |
| Time to pay off | 35 months | 27 months | 29 months | 27 months |

- **The 18-month offer saves $1,658.65** and finishes eight months sooner.
- **A longer promotion is not automatically cheaper.** The 21-month offer leaves less to pay interest on, but its higher fee makes it cost more than the 18-month one.
- **To clear the $5,150 before 18 months are up** you would pay $286.12 a month.

The comparison assumes the rate after the promotion matches your current APR and that you make no purchases on the new card. Check the go-to rate and the fee in the offer's terms.

# APR, Monthly Rate and Effective Rate

| For a 24% APR | Rate |
|---|---:|
| Daily periodic rate (÷ 365) | 0.06575% |
| 30-day cycle | 1.9726% |
| Monthly on a 360-day basis (÷ 12) | 2.0000% |
| Effective annual rate, with interest charged on interest | 26.83% |

The APR is the headline rate. Because unpaid interest joins the balance each month, carrying a balance for a full year costs more than the APR suggests.

# What Your Statement Has to Tell You

- **United States.** Under Regulation Z (the Credit CARD Act rules), statements must show a minimum payment warning, how long minimum-only payments would take and their total cost. In most cases they also show the monthly payment that would clear the balance in 36 months.
- **United Kingdom.** If over 18 months you paid more in interest, fees and charges than you repaid, FCA rules require your provider to contact you; after 36 months it must offer a way to repay in a reasonable period, and show forbearance if you cannot afford it.
