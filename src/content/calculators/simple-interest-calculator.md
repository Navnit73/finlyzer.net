# Simple Interest Formula

**SI = P × R × T ÷ 100**

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| SI | Simple interest |
| P | Principal: the amount lent, borrowed or deposited |
| R | Rate of interest per year, in percent |
| T | Time in years |
| A | Amount at the end = P + SI = P × (1 + R × T ÷ 100) |

Rearranged, the same formula finds any missing value:

| To find | Use |
|---|---|
| Interest | SI = P × R × T ÷ 100 |
| Principal | P = 100 × SI ÷ (R × T) |
| Rate | R = 100 × SI ÷ (P × T) |
| Time | T = 100 × SI ÷ (P × R) |

# Solved Simple Interest Problems

**1. Find the interest.** ₹50,000 is lent at 8% a year for 3 years.
SI = 50,000 × 8 × 3 ÷ 100 = **₹12,000**. Amount = 50,000 + 12,000 = ₹62,000.

**2. Find the principal.** A deposit earns ₹4,500 of simple interest at 7.5% a year in 3 years. How much was deposited?
P = 100 × 4,500 ÷ (7.5 × 3) = 4,50,000 ÷ 22.5 = **₹20,000**.

**3. Find the rate.** ₹20,000 earns ₹3,600 of interest in 2 years.
R = 100 × 3,600 ÷ (20,000 × 2) = 3,60,000 ÷ 40,000 = **9% a year**.

**4. Find the time.** How long does ₹15,000 take to earn ₹1,800 at 6% a year?
T = 100 × 1,800 ÷ (15,000 × 6) = 1,80,000 ÷ 90,000 = **2 years**.

**5. Time in months.** ₹20,000 at 9% a year for 8 months.
T = 8 ÷ 12 years, so SI = 20,000 × 9 × 8 ÷ 1,200 = **₹1,200**.

**6. Time in days.** ₹1,00,000 at 9% a year for 90 days.
SI = 1,00,000 × 9 × 90 ÷ 36,500 = **₹2,219.18**. On a 360-day year it is 1,00,000 × 9 × 90 ÷ 36,000 = ₹2,250.

**7. Rate per month.** ₹1,00,000 at 2% a month for 6 months.
R = 2 × 12 = 24% a year, T = 0.5, so SI = 1,00,000 × 24 × 0.5 ÷ 100 = **₹12,000**.

# Interest per Year, Month and Day

Simple interest builds up at the same speed throughout the loan or deposit. For ₹50,000 at 8% a year:

| Period | Interest |
|---|---:|
| Per year | ₹4,000.00 |
| Per month | ₹333.33 |
| Per day (÷ 365) | ₹10.96 |

That steady rate is why lenders can quote a daily interest figure for paying a loan off early: the interest so far is the daily amount times the days that have passed.

# Common Mistakes With the Formula

- **Time not in years.** T must be in years when R is a yearly rate. 8 months is 8 ÷ 12, not 8.
- **A monthly rate used as a yearly rate.** 2% a month is 24% a year. Mixing them up understates the interest twelve times.
- **Percent entered as a decimal, or both.** The ÷ 100 in the formula already converts the percent. Use R = 8, not 0.08, with this version of the formula.
- **Interest confused with the amount.** SI is only the interest. The amount A = P + SI is what is repaid or received at the end.
- **Counting both the first and last day.** Between two dates, count the start day but not the end day: 1 March to 30 May is 90 days.

# Simple Interest vs Compound Interest

Simple interest is earned only on the principal. Compound interest is also earned on interest already added. For ₹50,000 at 8% a year, with yearly compounding:

| Years | Simple interest | Compound interest | Difference |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | ₹4,000.00 | ₹4,000.00 | ₹0.00 |
| 3 | ₹12,000.00 | ₹12,985.60 | ₹985.60 |
| 5 | ₹20,000.00 | ₹23,466.40 | ₹3,466.40 |
| 10 | ₹40,000.00 | ₹57,946.25 | ₹17,946.25 |
| 20 | ₹80,000.00 | ₹1,83,047.86 | ₹1,03,047.86 |

The two are equal after one year. After 20 years, compound interest has earned more than twice as much. The [compound interest calculator](/calculators/compound-interest-calculator) handles monthly compounding and regular deposits.

# Where You Meet Simple Interest

- **Flat-rate loans.** Some vehicle and personal loans charge interest on the original amount for the whole term, even as it is repaid. Their real cost is far higher than the quoted rate.
- **Interest calculated daily on a balance.** Many car loans and short-term business loans charge each day's interest on the outstanding principal only, so paying early saves interest.
- **Deposits that pay out interest.** A deposit that pays its interest to you each quarter, rather than adding it to the balance, earns simple interest on the original amount.
- **Money market instruments and late-payment interest,** which are usually calculated per day on a fixed amount.

The calculator applies the formula exactly as written. Check your loan or deposit agreement for the day count and how often interest is charged or paid.
