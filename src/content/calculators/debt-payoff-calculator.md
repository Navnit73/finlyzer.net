# What Is a Credit Card Debt Payoff Calculator?

A credit card debt payoff calculator shows how long it will take to clear what you owe and how much interest you will pay on the way. This one handles several cards and loans together. List each with its balance, APR and minimum payment, enter the total you can pay each month, and it decides which debt gets the extra money, moves the payment along as each one is cleared, and gives you the debt-free date for the avalanche and snowball methods. Add a one-off payment if you are expecting a bonus or refund.

# Example: Paying Off Three Credit Cards

Three cards with $8,000 owed in total and $500 a month to pay them, $285 above the $215 of minimums:

| Card | Balance | APR | Minimum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Card A | $4,000 | 24% | $100 |
| Card B | $1,500 | 18% | $40 |
| Card C | $2,500 | 27% | $75 |

| | Avalanche | Snowball | Minimums only ($215) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Debt-free in | 20 months | 20 months | 69 months |
| Total interest | $1,623.94 | $1,778.69 | $6,768.72 |
| Order cleared | C (month 8), A (17), B (20) | B (5), C (12), A (20) | |

Both methods finish in the same month here, but avalanche costs $154.75 less because the 27% card goes first. In month 1 avalanche pays $360 to Card C and the minimums to the others; snowball pays $325 to Card B, the smallest balance. Paying $500 instead of the minimums saves over $5,000 of interest and more than four years.

# Avalanche or Snowball: Which Clears Your Cards Faster?

Each month the calculator adds interest to every unpaid debt (**balance × APR ÷ 12**), pays every minimum, and sends the rest of your budget to one priority debt:

- **Avalanche:** the highest APR first. It always costs the least interest for a fixed budget.
- **Snowball:** the smallest balance first. You see whole debts disappear sooner, which keeps many people going.

**Extra payment = monthly budget − minimums on the debts still unpaid.** When a debt is cleared its minimum stays in the budget, so the extra payment grows: that rollover is the snowball (or avalanche) effect. If the two methods come out close for your debts, pick the one you are more likely to stick with.

# What a One-Off Payment Does

A windfall paid onto the priority debt brings every later payoff date forward. Add $1,000 in month 4 to the three-card example:

| | Avalanche | Avalanche + $1,000 in month 4 | Snowball + $1,000 in month 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Debt-free in | 20 months | 17 months | 17 months |
| Total interest | $1,623.94 | $1,301.06 | $1,421.90 |

The $1,000 saves $322.88 of interest and three months. Under avalanche it lands on the 27% card, which is then cleared in month 5 instead of month 8.

# Ways to Pay Off Credit Card Debt

| Strategy | How it works | Best when |
|---|---|---|
| Avalanche | Extra money to the highest APR first | You want the lowest total interest |
| Snowball | Extra money to the smallest balance first | You want quick wins to stay motivated |
| Balance transfer | Move balances to a card with a low introductory rate | You can clear the balance before the promotional rate ends |
| Consolidation loan | Replace several cards with one fixed-rate loan | The loan's APR, including fees, is lower than your cards' |

Balance transfers usually charge a fee, a percentage of the amount moved, and the rate rises when the promotion ends; the [credit card interest calculator](/calculators/credit-card-interest-calculator) compares a transfer offer against staying put. Use the [loan comparison calculator](/calculators/loan-comparison-calculator) to check that a consolidation loan's fees do not cancel the saving.

# Managing Several Cards While You Pay Them Down

- **Automate every minimum.** A missed payment can bring a late fee and a penalty APR that undoes months of progress. Set up automatic minimum payments and send the extra by hand.
- **Line up the due dates.** Many issuers let you change your due date; having them close together makes one payday cover all of them.
- **Stop adding to the balances.** New purchases push every payoff date back. Use a debit card while the plan runs.
- **Raise the budget when you can,** even slightly. Extra money reduces the balance that interest is charged on, so the saving compounds.
- **Recheck the plan when a rate or balance changes,** and download a fresh spreadsheet.

# Using It With Your Card Statements

US card statements include a minimum payment warning showing how long minimum-only payments would take and what they would cost. Compare it with your plan to see what the extra payments are worth.

The calculator is a planning tool, not financial advice. If you cannot cover your minimum payments, contact your lenders or a nonprofit credit counselor before you miss a payment.
