# EMI per ₹1 Lakh by Interest Rate and Tenure

Multiply the figure by your loan amount in lakh for a quick estimate: a ₹30 lakh loan at 9% for 20 years is about 30 × ₹900 = ₹27,000 a month.

These are reducing-balance EMIs rounded to the rupee. The calculator gives the exact figure to the paisa, the same EMI per ₹1 lakh for your own loan, and the full schedule.

# The EMI Formula, Step by Step

**EMI = P × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1)**

P is the loan amount, r the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n the number of EMIs. For a ₹25,00,000 home loan at 8.5% over 20 years:

1. r = 8.5 ÷ 12 ÷ 100 = 0.0070833
2. n = 20 × 12 = 240
3. (1 + r)^n = 1.0070833^240 ≈ 5.44
4. EMI = 25,00,000 × 0.0070833 × 5.44 ÷ 4.44 ≈ **₹21,695.58**

Over 240 EMIs you repay ₹52,06,939.76: the ₹25,00,000 borrowed plus ₹27,06,939.76 of interest.

In Excel or Google Sheets, **=PMT(8.5%/12, 240, -2500000)** gives the same 21,695.58. At a 0% rate, the EMI is simply P ÷ n.

# Where Each EMI Goes

Interest is charged on the balance still owed, so early EMIs are mostly interest. Of the first ₹21,695.58, ₹17,708.33 is interest and ₹3,987.25 repays the loan. After 10 years, halfway through, ₹17,49,845.75 is still outstanding: under a third of the loan has been repaid.

That is why tenure has a bigger effect on cost than on the EMI:

| Tenure | EMI | Total interest |
|---|---:|---:|
| 10 years | ₹30,996.42 | ₹12,19,570.80 |
| 15 years | ₹24,618.49 | ₹19,31,327.77 |
| 20 years | ₹21,695.58 | ₹27,06,939.76 |
| 25 years | ₹20,130.68 | ₹35,39,201.03 |
| 30 years | ₹19,222.84 | ₹44,20,217.75 |

Stretching 20 years to 30 lowers the EMI by ₹2,472.74 and adds ₹17,13,277.99 of interest.

# Your EMI by Loan Type

The formula is the same for every loan. What you enter, and what the EMI leaves out, differs.

## Home loan

Enter the loan amount, not the property price. If the property is under construction and the loan is released in stages, you pay **pre-EMI** (interest only) on what has been disbursed: amount disbursed × rate ÷ 12. On ₹10,00,000 disbursed at 8.5%, that is ₹7,083.33 a month, and it does not reduce the loan. Add your first EMI month in the calculator to get the schedule by financial year, the basis lenders use for interest certificates.

## Car loan

Some lenders collect the first EMI at disbursal ("EMI in advance") rather than a month later. That slightly lowers the EMI because you borrow for one month less; the calculator assumes the usual monthly-in-arrears schedule, so check the sanction letter.

## Personal loan

Processing fees are usually deducted from the amount paid out, so you receive less than you repay on. Some lenders quote a flat rate: ask for the reducing-balance rate before using the calculator, and compare offers on APR with the [loan comparison calculator](/calculators/loan-comparison-calculator).

## Education loan

Repayment often starts after a moratorium (the course plus a grace period). Interest can build up during that time, so enter the amount you will owe when EMIs begin, not the amount first disbursed.

# When Your EMI Changes

Home loans often carry a floating rate. When the rate rises, many lenders keep the EMI and add instalments, which is easy to miss. For the example loan, with the change at the start:

| New rate | EMI for the same 20 years | EMIs at the old ₹21,695.58 |
|---|---:|---:|
| 9.0% (+0.5) | ₹22,493.15 | 268 (+28) |
| 9.5% (+1.0) | ₹23,303.28 | 309 (+69) |

RBI's rules for EMI-based personal loans, which include home loans to individuals, require the lender to tell you about the change and let you choose a higher EMI, a longer tenure, a mix of the two, or a switch to a fixed rate where the lender offers one. Try any change in the "If a floating rate changes" box.

# Ways to Pay Less

- **Choose a shorter tenure** if the EMI fits. It is the biggest single saving (see the table above).
- **Pay more upfront.** Every ₹1 lakh less borrowed cuts the EMI by the amount in the per-lakh table.
- **Prepay when you can.** For floating-rate loans to individuals sanctioned or renewed on or after 1 January 2026, lenders may not charge for prepayment. Keeping the EMI and shortening the tenure usually saves the most; the [loan repayment calculator](/calculators/loan-repayment-calculator) shows the interest saved by part-payments.
- **Compare on APR.** Since October 2024, lenders must give a Key Facts Statement with the annual percentage rate (APR), which includes fees, for retail loans. A lower headline rate with higher fees can cost more.

# Reading the Schedule by Financial Year

If your first EMI is in November 2026, the 2026-27 financial year (April to March) contains only 5 EMIs. They pay ₹88,257.23 of interest and ₹20,220.67 of principal. FY 2027-28 is the first full year: ₹2,08,803.92 of interest and ₹51,543.04 of principal. The loan then runs across 21 financial years, ending with 7 EMIs in FY 2046-47.

These are the figures that match the yearly interest certificate your lender issues, which is why the financial-year view helps at tax time. Lenders' schedules can differ by a few rupees because of the first-month (broken-period) interest and rate resets; your lender's statement is the binding figure.
