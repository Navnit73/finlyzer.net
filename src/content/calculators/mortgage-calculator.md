# How to Use the Mortgage Calculator

1. **Enter the home price and down payment.** Switch between a dollar amount and a percentage; the other figure is shown under the field.
2. **Enter the interest rate and term.** Use the interest rate from your Loan Estimate, not the APR, which also includes fees.
3. **Add taxes, insurance and fees.** Property tax can be a yearly amount or a percentage of the price. Leave anything that doesn't apply at 0.
4. **Enter the PMI rate** if you put down less than 20%. The calculator only charges PMI while the loan-to-value is above the federal thresholds.
5. **Read the monthly payment** and the breakdown, then open the amortization schedule to see every payment.

The figures loaded in the calculator are an example, not current market rates.

# Mortgage Payment Formula

**Monthly principal and interest = L × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1)**

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| L | Loan amount = home price − down payment |
| r | Monthly rate = annual interest rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100 |
| n | Number of payments = years × 12 |

**Total monthly payment (PITI) = principal and interest + property tax ÷ 12 + insurance ÷ 12 + PMI + HOA dues**

Each month, interest is charged on the remaining balance and the rest of the principal and interest payment reduces the loan. The calculator rounds each month's interest to the cent, so the final payment can differ by a few dollars.

# Mortgage Payment Example

A $400,000 home with 10% down, a 30-year fixed loan at 6.5%, property tax of 1.1% of the price, $1,800 a year of homeowners insurance and PMI at 0.5% of the loan:

| Item | Monthly |
|---|---:|
| Principal and interest ($360,000 at 6.5%, 360 payments) | $2,275.44 |
| Property tax ($4,400 ÷ 12) | $366.67 |
| Homeowners insurance ($1,800 ÷ 12) | $150.00 |
| PMI (0.5% × $360,000 ÷ 12) | $150.00 |
| **Total monthly payment** | **$2,942.11** |

Over 30 years the loan costs $459,164.00 in interest, more than the amount borrowed. In the first year, $23,281.54 of the $27,305.28 you pay in principal and interest is interest; only $4,023.74 reduces the balance.

With 20% down instead, the loan is $320,000, there is no PMI, and the monthly payment falls to $2,539.29.

# When PMI Ends

Private mortgage insurance protects the lender, not you, when you borrow more than 80% of the home's value on a conventional loan. Federal law (the Homeowners Protection Act) sets when it stops:

| Point | What happens |
|---|---|
| Balance scheduled to reach 80% of original value | You can ask your servicer in writing to cancel PMI |
| Balance scheduled to reach 78% of original value | PMI ends automatically if you are current on payments |
| Midpoint of the loan term | PMI ends even if the balance is still above 78% |

In the example, the scheduled balance falls to 80% ($320,000) after payment 95 and to 78% ($312,000) after payment 109. You pay PMI 109 times, $16,350 in total, unless you ask to cancel it from payment 96. These dates follow the original amortization schedule; paying extra can let you request cancellation sooner, but does not move the automatic 78% date. Some high-risk loans follow different rules, so check the PMI disclosure in your closing documents.

# What Affects Your Mortgage Payment

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Interest rate | The biggest driver of interest cost; each change moves every payment for the life of a fixed loan |
| Term | Longer terms lower the payment but add interest |
| Down payment | Lowers the loan amount and, at 20% or more, removes PMI |
| Property tax | Set by your county or city and can change every year |
| Insurance and HOA | Vary by location, coverage and community |

# Escrow and Your Loan Estimate

Most lenders collect property tax and insurance with your mortgage payment and hold them in escrow. Your Loan Estimate shows the principal and interest, mortgage insurance and estimated escrow as separate lines; enter those figures here to check the total and to compare loan offers.

This calculator is for estimates. Your lender's Loan Estimate and Closing Disclosure are the binding figures.
