# How to Use the Loan Repayment Calculator

The calculator has two modes. Pick the one that matches what you know.

**The loan term.** Enter the amount borrowed, the interest rate and the term. You get the monthly repayment and payoff date. Then add overpayments:

1. **Extra every month:** a fixed amount on top of every repayment.
2. **One-off overpayments:** lump sums such as a bonus or tax refund, each in the month you plan to pay it.

The result compares the loan with and without overpayments: time saved, interest saved and the new payoff date.

**What I can pay.** Enter the balance you owe now, the rate and the monthly amount you can afford. You get the number of payments, the total interest and the size of the final payment.

# Loan Repayment Formulas

**Monthly repayment = B × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1)**

**Number of payments = −ln(1 − r × B ÷ P) ÷ ln(1 + r)**, rounded up

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| B | Balance (amount borrowed or still owed) |
| r | Monthly rate = annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100 |
| n | Number of monthly repayments |
| P | Monthly payment |

The second formula only works when P is larger than r × B, the first month's interest. Each month the calculator charges interest on the balance (rounded to the penny), takes the repayment and any overpayment off the balance, and stops when it reaches zero.

# Worked Example: Overpaying a £15,000 Loan

A £15,000 loan at 7.9% a year over 5 years has a monthly repayment of £303.43 and costs £3,205.71 in interest over 60 payments.

| Scenario | Payments | Interest | Saved |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| No overpayments | 60 | £3,205.71 | |
| £100 extra a month | 43 | £2,260.76 | 17 months, £944.95 |
| £100 a month + £1,000 in month 12 | 40 | £2,044.98 | 20 months, £1,160.73 |

In the last scenario you overpay £4,907.78 in total and are debt-free 20 months early. The overpayments do not cost you anything extra: you pay £1,160.73 less overall.

# Worked Example: Paying What You Can Afford

The same £15,000 at 7.9%, paid at a fixed amount each month:

| Monthly payment | Payments needed | Total interest |
|---:|---:|---:|
| £300 | 61 | £3,252.93 |
| £400 | 44 | £2,283.59 |

Paying £400 instead of £300 clears the debt 17 months sooner and saves £969.34 of interest. A payment of £98.75 or less never clears it, because that is the first month's interest.

# What Changes Your Payoff Date

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Overpayment size | Every extra pound shortens the loan |
| Timing | Earlier overpayments save more interest than later ones |
| Interest rate | The higher the rate, the more each overpayment saves |
| Remaining term | Overpaying early in a long loan saves the most, because most of the interest is still to come |

# Before You Overpay

- **Check the agreement.** Fixed-rate loans and mortgages can carry an early repayment charge. In the UK, compensation on regulated consumer credit not secured on property is capped by law; mortgages follow the terms of your mortgage offer, which may allow some overpayment each year without a charge.
- **Tell the lender what the money is for.** Ask for overpayments to reduce the balance and shorten the term, not to pay future instalments in advance.
- **Clear expensive debt first.** Overpaying a 7.9% loan makes little sense while a credit card charges more.
- **Keep an emergency fund.** Money paid into a loan is hard to get back.

The calculator assumes a fixed interest rate and that the monthly repayment stays the same. Your lender's settlement figure is the binding amount.
